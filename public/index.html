Methodology & Calibration

How we audit 35 global newsrooms every day, separate verified real-world impact from headline rhetoric, and surface genuine human progress.

🧭

Why Two Editions? ("No Good News" vs. "Some Good News")

Commercial media operates as a 24-hour threat-surveillance engine. Because human attention is evolutionarily wired to fixate on danger, news desks disproportionately amplify conflict, crisis, and outrage—leaving readers with the false impression that nothing constructive is happening on Earth.

No Good News audits this structural negativity across 35 major international outlets using their raw, un-personalized RSS feeds. Some Good News uses that exact same dataset to rescue the verified scientific, medical, environmental, and humanitarian breakthroughs that get buried beneath the daily panic.

The 4-Factor First-Principles Formula

Asymmetric Continuum: -1.00 to +1.00

Simple keyword dictionaries fail at journalism, and asking an LLM to guess a single rounded sentiment float causes artificial clustering around numbers like -0.75 or +0.30. Instead, Gemini Flash evaluates every dispatch across four orthogonal, higher-order dimensions—with zero artificial topic caps—which are mathematically combined using an asymmetric impact curve:

Final Score (-1.00 to +1.00) = Polarity × AsymmetricImpact(Reach, Permanence) + Editorial Framing

1. Real-World Event Base (-0.92 to +0.90)

Computed continuously from three universal physical properties of the underlying event—with an asymmetric magnitude curve reflecting that progress is slow to build while destruction is acute:

  • Polarity & Actuality (-100 to +100): Evaluates the inherent human valence of verified real-world events, physical discoveries, and enacted decisions—while discounting unsubstantiated forecasts, self-interested corporate promotion, and pure op-ed rhetoric toward neutral (0).
  • Reach (0 to 100): The breadth of the population, institution, or ecosystem affected—scaling smoothly from an individual or single firm up to national and planetary scope.
  • Permanence & Asymmetric Scaling (0 to 100): Constructive progress (Polarity ≥ 0) requires both broad Reach and lasting Permanence (R0.60 · D0.40) so ephemeral corporate PR or local human-interest blips don't masquerade as breakthroughs. Destructive harm (Polarity < 0) uses a Hill saturation curve (R0.72 · D0.28) because violent unrest, attacks, and disasters are immediately severe even when short-lived.

2. Editorial Framing (-0.18 to +0.10)

Rated continuously (-100 to +100) by comparing how this specific newsroom packaged the headline against a strictly neutral wire-service statement (0):

  • Sensationalist / Conflict-Amplifying (-): Uses emotionally loaded conflict language, foregrounds worst-case dread, or amplifies partisan blame and fear beyond the underlying facts.
  • Proportional Wire Baseline (0.00): Direct, matter-of-fact reporting of verified facts without emotional spin or speculative hype.
  • Constructive / Composed (+): Foregrounds verified human agency, cooperation, resilience, context, or solutions without sugarcoating facts.

Three Calibration Guardrails

🔗 Cross-Batch & Cross-Sync Event Coherence

Dispatches covering the same developing story across all 35 outlets are grouped into shared evaluation batches and anchored against a 36-hour rolling event memory—locking objective Polarity, Reach, and Permanence across newsrooms while isolating each editor's Framing.

⚖️ Counterfactual Harm & Epistemic Actuality

We ask: "If this entire situation had never happened, would the world be safer or better?" Criminal convictions, murder trials, and crisis bailouts remain negative reactions to harm—while unverified corporate claims and speculative op-eds are separated from concrete real-world actions.

🧠 Pure Semantic AI Clustering

Developing story clusters are grouped 100% semantically by Gemini reading each headline and lead summary—with zero brittle keyword rules—selecting one definitive dispatch per newsroom so live-blog spam never skews the spectrum.

Control Experiment: Is the AI Cynical, or Is the News?

To verify that our grading engine isn't biased toward negativity, we ran the exact same Gemini rubric against a control feed from The Good News Network (GNN) and a 46-story stratified benchmark across all 10 news beats.

While the 35 mainstream newsrooms average -0.21, the control feed scored +0.73—proving the model responds symmetrically when fed constructive journalism.
