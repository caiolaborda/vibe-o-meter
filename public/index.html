Methodology & Calibration

How we audit 17 global newsrooms every day, separate real-world impact from headline spin, and surface genuine human progress.

🧭

Why Two Editions? ("No Good News" vs. "Some Good News")

Commercial media operates as a 24-hour threat-surveillance engine. Because human attention is evolutionarily wired to fixate on danger, news desks disproportionately amplify conflict, crisis, and outrage—leaving readers with the false impression that nothing constructive is happening on Earth.

No Good News audits this structural negativity across 17 major international outlets using their raw, un-personalized RSS feeds. Some Good News uses that exact same dataset to rescue the verified scientific, medical, environmental, and humanitarian breakthroughs that get buried beneath the daily panic.

The 2-Step Gemini Evaluation Formula

Continuous Scale: -1.00 to +1.00

Simple keyword dictionaries fail at journalism because words like "strike" or "crash" appear in routine reporting. Instead, every headline and lead summary is evaluated by Gemini 3.8 Flash by summing two distinct components:

Final Score (-1.00 to +1.00) = Event Base (-0.85 to +0.85) + Editorial Framing (-0.15 to +0.15)

Step 1: Event Base (-0.85 to +0.85)

Measures the inherent real-world impact of the event strictly by how many lives it affects and how lasting the consequence is—never by topic labels:

  • Civilizational / Mass-Scale (±0.65 to ±0.85): Active warfare or mass casualties vs. historic peace accords, disease eradication, or foundational scientific breakthroughs.
  • Societal / Multi-Year (±0.35 to ±0.60): Severe disasters or institutional breakdown vs. national cost-of-living relief, binding treaties, coral/species recovery, or major archaeological discoveries.
  • Organizational / Transitory (±0.10 to ±0.30): Routine partisan friction or stock dips vs. single-company quarterly revenue beats, IPOs, or sports wins (capped below +0.28 so corporate profits never outrank human breakthroughs).

Step 2: Editorial Framing (-0.15 to +0.15)

Measures how a specific newsroom chose to phrase the headline compared to a neutral wire-service statement of the same fact:

  • Alarmist / Conflict-Amplifying (-0.15 to -0.05): Loaded conflict verbs ("slams", "chaos", "betrayed", "crisis") or worst-case speculation.
  • Matter-of-Fact / Wire Baseline (0.00): Direct, unembellished reporting of the event.
  • Constructive / Composed (+0.05 to +0.15): Highlights verified resilience, cooperation, de-escalation, or solutions.

Three Calibration Guardrails

🔗 Cross-Outlet Batch Parity

Headlines from all 17 outlets are interleaved into shared context batches. When 6 outlets cover the same event, they receive the exact same Event Base and diverge solely by each editor's Framing Modifier.

⚖️ Counterfactual Harm Rule

Criminal convictions, prison sentences, and corporate fines are reactions to underlying harm. An aspirin treats a headache, but the headache is still bad—so crime trials and penalties stay negative (-0.20 to -0.55), never false "good news."

📡 Unfiltered RSS Baseline

Instead of scraping personalized social feeds (X, TikTok) that optimize for individual outrage, we ingest each newsroom's primary RSS feed—capturing their true front-page editorial priority.

Control Experiment: Is the AI Cynical, or Is the News?

To verify that our grading engine isn't biased toward negativity, we ran the exact same Gemini rubric against a control feed from The Good News Network (GNN).

While the 17 mainstream newsrooms average -0.21, the control feed scored +0.73—proving the model responds symmetrically when fed constructive journalism.
